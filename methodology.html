How we test

Same data, same rules, for every bot. Here is exactly what goes into a report.

Data

Binance history

Futures 1-minute candles since 2020, funding rates and premium index for 40+ coins from Binance's public archive, topped up every 30 minutes. Spot candles at 5m to 1d for strategy backtests.

Second-level prices

For the last three months we also keep 1-second spot candles, so a claimed trade can be checked against the exact second it was supposedly filled.

Strategy backtests

  • Engine: Freqtrade backtesting, using the strategy's own ROI, stop-loss and exit rules.
  • Account: 1,000 USDT, up to 5 trades at once, unless the customer asks for something else.
  • Fees: 0.1% per side (Binance spot taker), plus a stress test at double fees.
  • Lookahead check: Freqtrade's lookahead analysis re-runs signals with future candles hidden. If the signals change, the strategy is using information it could not have had live.
  • Results are split by calendar year and by market condition (BTC 30-day trend up, down or sideways).

Track-record checks

  • Each entry and exit is compared with the real 1-minute candle, and with 1-second candles when available.
  • Each exchange and broker has slightly different prices, so we allow a margin: 0.15% around Binance for crypto and 0.05% around Dukascopy's bid/ask for Forex. A price outside that is marked "not consistent with the reference data", a reason to ask questions rather than proof of fraud. A price that only fits a minute or two earlier or later is marked as a timing difference. Broker time zones (such as GMT+2 or GMT+3) are detected automatically.
  • Profit is recalculated with taker fees and, for futures, the real funding payments.

Profit factor30%Gross profit clearly larger than gross loss, after fees

Sample size15%Enough trades to tell skill from luck (200+)

If the lookahead check finds future data, the score is capped at 30. If a few prices in a trade history do not match the reference data, it is capped at 60 (CAUTION); if 10% or more of the trades do, or one price is off by 2% or more, the report is a FAIL.

What a report is not

A backtest describes the past under stated assumptions. It is not a forecast and not investment advice. We test the past, not the future.
